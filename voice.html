07 · Brand voice

How we sound

D8A speaks like a sharp colleague, not a marketing department. Direct. Concrete. The lines below are from the live site. Prefer them over inventing new slogans.

Lines we actually say

  • The autonomous D8A platform.
  • Every person is a data person
  • Platform-agnostic DataOps
  • Specs are the new code
  • Human in command. Always.
  • Stop managing process. Start shipping data.
  • Define once. Deliver anywhere.
  • You set the mode
  • AI changed everything — except your data team’s workflow
  • Two products. One cascade. (Architect / Ship)

Three principles

01

Direct, not aggressive

Say what you mean in as few words as possible. Cut the preamble. Challenge the status quo, not the reader. “Human in command. Always.” is a boundary, not a punch.

02

Positive, not preachy

Frame around what teams can ship. “Ship data work weeks faster” beats “your process is broken.” The site names the 2018 workflow once, then moves to what D8A is.

03

Prove it, don’t proclaim it

Every claim needs a receipt: a screenshot, a cascade, a FAQ fact. Do not invent customer names, review volumes, or case studies. If it is not on d8a.pro or in this kit, do not say it.

We say / we don’t say

We say
“The autonomous D8A platform.”
The site H1. Product, not poetry. Use this as the headline unless a page has a more specific job.
We don’t say
“Empower your data teams to unlock synergies across the enterprise.”
Meaningless. Every word is a buzzword.
We say
“Specs are the new code.”
A claim you can demo: living spec, generated code, validated on save.
We don’t say
“We integrate seamlessly with your existing data ecosystem.”
“Seamlessly” is a red flag. Name the stack (ODBC, Databricks, Fabric) instead.
We say
“It removes process work, not engineers.”
The FAQ, verbatim. Use this whenever AI-replacement comes up.
We don’t say
“AI agents that replace your data team.”
The opposite of the product. Human in command is non-negotiable.
We say
“Stop managing process. Start shipping data.”
The close. Short, active, paired with the waitlist CTA.
We don’t say
“A comprehensive, end-to-end data operations management solution.”
Word soup. “Solution” is banned in headlines.

Tone

We sound like

  • A sharp technical colleague who cuts through noise
  • A product demo that speaks for itself
  • A well-written README — clear, structured, useful

We avoid sounding like

  • A marketing brochure full of superlatives
  • A consultant slide deck with more buzzwords than ideas
  • Generic AI-saas (purple gradients, “unlock”, “supercharge”)

LinkedIn & social

  • Open with a punch. Prefer a site line: “Specs are the new code.” / “Every person is a data person.”
  • One idea per post. Go deep on one cascade step, not the whole platform.
  • Show the product. Color-framed screenshot, window chrome, mono URL. The product is the proof.
  • End with a clear next step. Waitlist, d8a.pro, or a question about their stack. Not “thoughts?”
  • No hashtag spam. Two hashtags maximum.

Language rules

  • English only for external communication.
  • Sentence case for headings, buttons, labels. Not Title Case. Not ALL CAPS (except D8A and button tracking).
  • One thought per sentence. If it needs a semicolon, split it.
  • No emoji in product copy, docs, or formal comms. Allowed in casual social captions only. Never as icons.
  • Spell out acronyms once. First mention: “DataOps (Data Operations).” After that: “DataOps.”
  • Active voice. “D8A crystallizes a spec” not “A spec is crystallized by D8A.”
  • Do not invent proof. No fake logos, no fake metrics, no customer stories that are not in the record.
